# Craft Audit Prep

Two hours the day before, turned into one document he can hold during the call.

The point of the prep is stated in the SOP and worth repeating here: **walk in with the three things already drafted.** The ninety minutes confirms and sharpens them. A session that tries to discover them from nothing turns into a conversation, and a conversation is not worth what he paid.

## 0. Inputs

- The teardown. Run `builder-site-teardown` first if it does not exist
- Questionnaire answers from the funnel
- Fit Call notes or transcript. **This is the most important input**, because the three things have to be in his words and his words are in here
- The three things the confirmation video asked for: admin access, the photography invoice, and one project he lost this year that still bothers him
- Pricing from `07 Pricing and Payments` in the SOPs folder

If the Fit Call notes are missing, say so before doing anything else. Everything downstream degrades without them.

## 1. Business and buyer snapshot

Raw material for section 2 of the blueprint. From the questionnaire and the call, not from the website: homes a year, price band, build type, where work comes from and in what proportion, who buys and why, what he says makes him different.

Quote him. "We do the hard lots nobody else will bid" is usable in a blueprint. "Quality craftsmanship" is not, and if that is all there is, note that the differentiator is still unfound and make it a question for the call.

## 2. The lost project

He was asked for one project he lost this year that still bothers him. Treat it as the single most valuable thing in the file.

Write down: what it was, who he lost it to, what he thinks happened, and what a buyer would have seen on his site while deciding. If the site would have lost that job on its own merits, that is the opening of the ninety minutes and probably the first of the three things.

If he did not answer it, it is the first question at minute zero.

## 3. Competitors, three in his market

Find them the way a buyer would: search the way a buyer in that market searches, check who shows up in the map pack, check who the local magazines and parade of homes lists feature.

For each, record only what is comparable: how many homes have their own page, whether the process is explained, whether people are named, photography standard, review count and recency, and anything they publish that he does not.

Do not rank them against him on taste. Record what they publish. The useful sentence is "all three of these show the build, you show the finished kitchen", and that sentence needs three examples behind it.

## 4. Draft the three things

Exactly three. Rules:

- **His language, not yours.** Pull the phrasing from the Fit Call. Each one should sound like something he said
- **Each is something he is losing, not a feature he is missing.** "Buyers cannot tell you build at this level" beats "the site needs a projects section"
- **Each traceable to evidence in the teardown.** If there is no measurement or screenshot behind it, it is not ready
- **No more than three, and never a fourth in parentheses.** Three is the promise and the refund is tied to it

Write them as a table, with a column for the quote from the call and a column for the evidence:

| # | The thing, in his words | What he said on the call | Evidence from the teardown |

Then write, in one line each, what he does about it. That line becomes section 6.

## 5. The run sheet

Build the ninety minutes to the table in the Craft Audit SOP, filled in for this builder:

| Minutes | What | For this builder |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 10 | The business | The two or three things still unknown after the questionnaire |
| 10 to 35 | Walk the site together | The exact page order to open, and where to stop and let him react |
| 35 to 55 | How his homes should be presented | Which homes lead, in what order, and why |
| 55 to 70 | Everything else a buyer sees | Rights question, Google profile state, reviews |
| 70 to 85 | The sequence | The three things, confirmed, then ordered |
| 85 to 90 | What happens next | When the blueprint lands |

For the site walk, list the URLs in order with one line on what to say at each. Screen sharing while deciding what to open next is how twenty five minutes becomes forty.

**Do not pitch the build.** The blueprint does that in section 9, in writing. Put a line in the run sheet at minute 85 to say so out loud if he asks: the number is in the document, with the reasoning next to it.

## 6. Questions and risks

Two short lists:

**Must ask.** Anything the blueprint cannot be written without. Photography rights, who decides, how many homes he wants up at launch, whether there is a date he is working toward.

**Most likely to go sideways.** The honest one. If he says the site is fine, if he wants a number in the room, if the real problem turns out to be his sales process rather than his website. One line each on what to do. The last one is a refund conversation and the SOP for it is `10 When Things Go Wrong`.

`audits/{builder}/PREP.md`, dated:

```markdown

## The three things, drafted
| # | In his words | What he said | Evidence |

## Business and buyer snapshot

| Minutes | What | For this builder |

| # | URL | What to say |

## Most likely to go sideways

```

## 8. Report

Tell Andru: the three things in one line each, what is still missing before the call, and the single riskiest unknown. If the three things are not ready, say that plainly rather than shipping three weak ones. Three weak things is what a refund looks like.

House rules: no em dashes, his language not yours, never invent what he said, never put a price in the prep document.
